Group Project
Overview
The class project has three key objectives. First, it provides an opportunity to apply
the concepts and techniques covered in the module in a creative and practical context
(specific to your field of interest), supporting a deeper understanding of the material.
Second, it offers experience in designing and developing a unique project as part of a
team which is an essential skill that students are likely to encounter throughout their
academic and professional careers. Working collaboratively on a project also helps
develop communication, problem-solving, and project management skills. Third,
beyond the practical experience and creative satisfaction it offers, the project can
serve as a valuable addition to a student’s portfolio or résumé. Students may choose
to showcase their work publicly, for example through a GitHub repository or personal
website (optional)

Note about grading

There is no such thing as a “perfect project.” While you are encouraged to be
ambitious, the primary goal of this coursework is to support your learning experience.
You should avoid choosing a project that is too simple, but equally, you should not
select one that is clearly beyond the scope of the module or your current level of
expertise.

Keep in mind that more comprehensive and engaging projects often make it easier to
write a comprehensive 6 to 8 pages project report. However, the project proposal will
not be assessed based on how ambitious or exciting the project idea is. Instead,
evaluation will focus on how effectively you meet the objectives of the project proposal,
presentation, and final report.

Importantly, an unsuccessful outcome will not negatively impact your grade, provided
that you demonstrate honest reflection and critical analysis. For example, if you
choose to develop a classifier that does not achieve the desired accuracy, you will not
be penalised as long as you clearly explain the challenges encountered, analyse
possible reasons for the outcome, and propose meaningful improvements or future
experiments.

Ultimately, the objective of this coursework is to provide hands-on experience with AI
concepts and techniques while giving you the opportunity to learn through practical
application.

The project consists of four main components:

   1. Project proposal
   2. Short project presentation
   3. Project report

1) Project Proposal
Please note that you are required to use the proposal LaTeX file(s) when writing
and submitting your project proposal.

The main aim of the project proposal is to receive feedback from the instructor
regarding the feasibility of your project and whether it falls within the scope of this
course. In addition, the proposal provides an opportunity to obtain constructive
feedback and suggestions that may help strengthen your project.

For this project, you will work in teams of three or four students (Menti voting was
50:50). You are encouraged to form your own groups, as per your menti voting results.
If you are unable to find group members, the instructor will assign groups randomly. If
you experience concerns about working with someone in your group, please speak
with the instructor or the Demonstrator to discuss possible accommodations

Proposal Format:

   •   The project proposal should be 1–3 pages in length (800–1200 words),
       excluding references.
   •   You are required to use the LaTeX proposal template. This requirement serves
       two purposes:
          o   LaTeX is widely used in statistics, machine learning, and academic
              research, making it a valuable skill for your future work
          o   A standardized format ensures consistency across submissions, making
              grading more efficient and fairer.
       The proposal template is available in the proposal-latex files.
   •   You are encouraged, but not required, to include 1–2 figures to help illustrate
       technical concepts
   •   The proposal must be formatted and submitted as a PDF document. The
       submission deadline will be announced later through the course calendar and
       email

Introduction:

   •   Describe the project you plan to do
   •   Briefly discuss related research work, if applicable.

Motivation:

   •   Explain why your project is interesting or important. For example, you may
       discuss its broader societal impact or your personal learning goals and
       interests.

Evaluation:

   •   Describe what a successful outcome of your project would look like. In other
       words, under what conditions would you consider your project successful?
   •   Explain how success will be measured from a technical perspective, specifically
       for your project.

Resources:

   •   Describe the resources you plan to use, such as datasets (include URL),
       programming language, computational tools, software libraries, computer
       hardware, or other relevant materials

References:

   •   Include atleast 3 relevant published research work and include the references

Contributions:

All group members are expected to contribute equally to both the experimental and
writing components of the project. Although only one proposal and one final report will
be submitted per group, collaboration and coordination are essential.

Clearly specify the computational and writing responsibilities of each group
member.

Regular communication within your group is essential. You are strongly encouraged
to schedule consistent meetings and/or use communication platforms (e.g., Teams or
email) to stay connected throughout the semester and ensure steady progress on your
project.

Project Proposal Modification

After receiving feedback and your proposal grade, you are encouraged to follow your
original project outline as closely as possible. However, adjustments may be
appropriate if later course material introduces methods or concepts that better suit
your project (e.g., a more suitable AI model than the one originally proposed).

You are welcome to revise your proposal accordingly, though you will not be penalized
for keeping the original plan. If you would like to modify your project outline, please
consult the instructor or a Demonstrator first.

   4. Resources             [5 marks]

2) Project Presentation
During the second-last lecture of the module (24th July). Each group will present their
project to the class. The presentation format is flexible (“free form”), but it should
address the following key components:

   •   Introduce the topic to a general audience (i.e., your classmates).
   •   Summarize the main methodology or approach used in the project.
   •   Highlight and discuss the key outcomes and findings of the project.

15% Project presentation + Viva Q&A [Individual Score] – 24th July

Each presentation should be 15 or 20 minutes long depending on the number of
students in the group, followed by 5 minutes for questions and discussion. All group
members are expected to participate in the presentation

Project Presentation Assessment

The Project Presentation and Viva (Q&A session) accounts for 15% out of
the total 40% of the group project and will take place on 24th July.

Please note that while the presentation is delivered as a group, the
Presentation as well as the Viva will be assessed individually. This means
each student will receive an individual score based on their contribution to
the presentation and their ability to answer questions about the project during
the viva discussion.

Project Presentation Logistics


   • To encourage attendance and ensure fairness, the order of
     presentations will be determined randomly in class using a random
     number generator.

   • Presentation slides must be uploaded to QM+ at least one day before
     your scheduled presentation.

   • Please bring your own device for the presentation. The ITL Building
     classroom is equipped with USB-C and HDMI connections for the
     projector. However, you are strongly encouraged to visit the room in
     advance (ideally one week before your presentation) to test your setup
     and confirm that everything functions properly.

Project Presentation Assessment

The presentation will be graded out of 100 points according to the following rubric:


   a) 10 marks – Is the motivation for the project clearly explained?
   b) 40 marks – Is the project described clearly enough for a general audience with
      a background in AI to understand?
   c) 20 marks – Are figures and graphs clear, legible, and effectively explained?
   d) 20 marks – Are the results presented and discussed adequately?
   e) 10 points – Did all group members contribute meaningfully to the presentation?


In addition to the presentation itself, students should be prepared to answer questions
during the Viva Q&A, demonstrating their understanding of the project, methodology,
results, and their individual contributions.

3) Project Report
The project report is expected to be 6 to 8 pages in length (excluding references) and
should include the following sections:
   1. Introduction
   2. Related Work
   3. Proposed Methodology
   4. Implementation
   5. Results and Discussion
   6. Conclusions
   7. Individual Contributions

Additional guidance and formatting instructions are provided in the LaTeX report
template available in report-template.

Please note that you are required to use the report LaTeX file(s) when writing and
submitting your report.

In addition to the report, you must submit all code, computations, and experimental
materials developed and used throughout the project. These materials are required to
verify the originality and reproducibility of your work.

Please note that code quality will not directly affect your grade. The submitted code
will primarily serve to confirm that the results presented in the report are original,
reproducible, and based on actual experimentation.
