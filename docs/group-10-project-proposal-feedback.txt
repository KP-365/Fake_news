Suggestions for the ﬁnal report:

   •   It wasn’t very clear to me as to how the 3 layers will interact with each other (BERT-
       LoRA classiﬁcation, Google Fact Check API veriﬁcation, and explainable AI).
       Whether these layers are working independently, sequentially, or as an integrated
       hybrid framework.
   •   In ﬁnal report, add more detail about how conﬂicts between the classiﬁer
       prediction and external fact-checking results will be resolved. The explanation
       layer also requires further clariﬁcation, as it is unclear whether the natural
       language explanation agent will be based on a LLM, predeﬁned templates, or a
       rule-based approach.
   •   The dataset selection is appropriate; however, further justiﬁcation is required
       regarding the di;erences between LIAR and WELFake. LIAR contains short
       political claims, while WELFake contains longer news articles, and it is unclear
       how the proposed approach will handle these di;erences. The binary label
       conversion strategy for the LIAR dataset should also be explained in more detail,
       as categories such as “barely true” and “half true” may introduce ambiguity when
       converted into real and fake classes.

   •   Add details about dataset splitting (training/validation/testing), preprocessing
       steps, and how class imbalance will be handled.

   •   Explain why BERT with LoRA was selected over other transformer-based models
       or parameter-e;icient ﬁne-tuning approaches.

   •   In the ﬁnal report, discuss previous fake news detection approaches and their
       limitations, particularly methods based only on text classiﬁcation without
       veriﬁcation or uncertainty estimation.

   •   Include a clear architecture diagram/ﬂowchart showing the interaction between
       the three layers: BERT-LoRA classiﬁcation, fact-check retrieval, uncertainty
       estimation, and explanation generation.

    and classiﬁer-only versus the complete three-layer framework, to demonstrate
